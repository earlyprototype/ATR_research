## 1. What this project is, and what it has actually found

### The experiment, in one picture

Take a language model. Feed it a prompt, record its internal state (a list of about a thousand numbers that the model passes from layer to layer as it works), feed that state back into the model as if it were new input, and repeat, holding the overall signal strength constant. Keep going until the state stops changing. This is the project's core procedure, and the founding analogy describes it well: feed a tone into a room, record the echo, feed the echo back in, and repeat, until the room settles into the note it prefers.

The founding observation, made in Stage 1 of the project (recorded in the separate public repository), was that different models settle differently. GPT-2 Small, a 12-layer model, settles into a handful of distinct, meaningful, prompt-dependent states: on the reference set of 25 prompts, 13 settle on the word fragment "prolet" (as in proletariat), 3 on "till", 2 on "Anarch", and 7 circle forever around "Divine" without ever settling. GPT-2 Medium, a 24-layer model, does something starkly different: every prompt collapses into one state that reads out as the single bare letter "D", a near-meaningless token. A third model, Pythia-410m, neither settles nor collapses; it fragments. Small room, several clear notes; medium room, one dull hum; third room, noise. That contrast is what Stage 2 (this repository) set out to explain, using a theory borrowed from a published Anthropic paper: that a model's layers are organised into bands (an input-parsing band, a workspace band, an output band), and that the looping procedure was reading out that band structure.

### What Stage 2 actually did

Since 2026-07-23, a fleet of automated sessions has run the looping procedure through every variation the plan called for and several it did not. The centrepiece is a complete map: for the medium model, every possible way of cutting the loop was tested. "Cutting the loop" means choosing which layer the fed-back signal is injected into (call it layer i) and which layer the state is read back out of (call it layer j). For a 24-layer model there are 300 valid (i, j) pairs, and all 300 have now been measured at the registered protocol, 25 prompts each, 7,500 runs in total, of which 7,435 settled (99.1 percent). I reran the analysis over the committed run files today and every count below reproduces exactly.

### The two findings that changed the meaning of the founding observation

**First: the collapse is a one-cell phenomenon.** Across all 300 ways of cutting the loop, the "D" collapse occurs in exactly one: the full-stack cut, inject at layer 0 and read out at layer 23. Even the nearest neighbours of that cut (inject at layer 1, 2, or 3 instead of 0) produce something else entirely. Chance gives no baseline here because this is a census, not a sample: it is simply a fact of the complete map that 1 cell in 300 produces the famous collapse.

**Second: the collapse requires the apparatus to shout.** A control experiment (EXP_010c-VARIANTS in the register, run 2026-07-25) measured how loud the fed-back signal actually is under the registered convention, relative to the natural strength of a signal entering that layer. For the full-stack cut on Medium the answer is roughly 220 times natural; the committed record puts it at 217.8 times. (An independent recomputation gives about 235 times; same order, nothing turns on it.) The same control then turned the loudness down to natural and reran the baseline: the collapse vanished. Not one of the 25 prompts settled, and the letter "D" appeared nowhere. A second control moved the injection point from layer 0 to layer 1, keeping the loudness: the collapse vanished again. So the founding collapse has been observed only under the joint condition of injecting at the very first layer and injecting at roughly two hundred times natural strength, and removing either condition removes it. This is established by committed artifacts, not inferred.

The small model's landscape carries the same caveat in a milder form: its full-stack loop injects at roughly 56 to 175 times natural strength (the committed energy table for the small-model window grid), and the natural-loudness version of the small model's landscape has never been run.

In the founding analogy: we have learned that the medium room's dull hum happens only when the echo is fed back through one particular door, at two hundred times the room's natural volume. The hum may be the sound of the door, not the room. And here is exactly where the analogy stops holding, so I will say so: a real room is linear, meaning that turning the volume up scales the echo but cannot change which notes the room prefers. A language model is not linear, and volume can create or destroy the settling behaviour outright, which is precisely what the control showed. In a real room the loudness could never have manufactured the hum; here it evidently can. The analogy is good for the loop and bad for the loudness.

### What else the map found

Three more results from the window map matter for what follows. First, a small number of window cells produce whole recognisable words that vary by prompt, the closest thing in Medium to the small model's behaviour: 21 cells out of 300 have this character (verified today by rerunning the analysis). The two flagship cells are inject-at-8, read-at-21 (called O8 in the record, terminals "simultaneously" and "halfway") and inject-at-10, read-at-21 (called A4, terminals "until", "forever", "since"). Second, these cells are isolated islands, not a band: the cell between the two flagships (inject at layer 9, same readout) produces the junk fragment "oooooooo" on 18 of 25 prompts and punctuation on the rest, and this refutation of contiguity was pre-registered and survived a test on a completely disjoint set of 25 prompts. Third, one register-level correction from the completed census: an earlier hand-forward note said readout depth 21 was the only reliable readout depth. The full census does not support that generalisation. Across the eleven census cells that read out at depth 21, the two independent readout methods agree on average on only 6.6 of 25 prompts, while at depth 22 they agree on 18.9 of 25. The two flagship cells themselves (17 of 25 and 23 of 25 agreement) were not re-measured and still stand, but the column-wide prior built on them is dead, and the register's erratum (d) records this.

## 2. The apparatus-mask hypothesis, now the central question

The hypothesis, recorded and properly fenced as untested in the session note of 2026-07-27, is this: the founding cross-model contrast (small model settles into meaning, medium model collapses into emptiness) may be a property of the injection convention, the apparatus, rather than of the models. Three observations jointly motivate it.

1. The collapse is apparatus-conditional, as section 1 established: layer-0 injection and two-hundred-fold loudness are both required.
2. Underneath the collapse, structure resembling the small model's is already present. This is the strangest single result in the project. The medium model's full-stack terminal states, the very states that all read out as the letter "D", group the 25 prompts into clusters that resemble the small model's clusters far more than chance. The agreement score is 0.200 on a scale where 0 means no better than random grouping and 1 means identical grouping; of 10,000 random regroupings, about 9 matched or beat it (p = 0.0009). I verified both numbers in the committed comparison file today, along with the sweep showing the result holds across three of four clustering thresholds. Meanwhile the escaped window A4 (inject 10, read 21), the one that produces real words, resembles the small model no better than chance (score 0.048, where about 1 in 4 random regroupings do as well). So escaping the collapse does not recover the small model's geometry; it manufactures a different one. The hidden resemblance sits under the mask.
3. The word-producing windows have no independent corroboration. A second, independently built instrument (the Jacobian lens from the Anthropic paper, a different way of reading internal states into words) was fitted to the medium model and pointed at the same layers. It found no band and no islands: under the pre-registered dominance rule, zero of the 23 layers qualified, and layers 8, 9, and 10 are indistinguishable from each other and from their flanks under every metric the lens records (verified today in the lens census file). On top of that, a placebo run of the same window grid on Pythia-410m, a model with no consolidation behaviour, found window-position effects there too, so window effects as such are generic to this kind of model and prove nothing about workspaces by themselves.

Put together: the theory Stage 2 set out to test (band structure explains the contrast) has lost nearly all of its supporting legs, while the suspicion that the apparatus itself painted the founding picture has gained three. That is why the mask hypothesis is now the centre of the project.

It is a hypothesis, not a finding. The alternatives recorded with it are still live: the cluster resemblance could reflect shallow prompt features (length, syntax) that any two models sharing the same scheme for chopping text into units might agree on; the agreement score is a proxy for shared geometry, not proof of it; and the comparison rests on one 25-prompt set, with 7 of the 25 reference prompts never having settled on the small-model side.

The decisive next test is already chartered as issue #59, and both of its outcomes are informative. The repository holds the medium model's terminal states recorded at natural loudness (from the control that abolished the collapse) and the small model's reference grouping over the same 25 prompts. Comparing them is pure arithmetic on committed files, no model compute. If the resemblance to the small model survives at natural loudness, the mask hypothesis strengthens, because the structure does not depend on the shouting. If it drops to chance, the mask hypothesis weakens, because the structure was bound to the loud convention all along. One caveat travels with the test and is written into the charter: at natural loudness the medium model's runs do not settle (0 of 25 reached the stability criterion), so the comparison necessarily uses unsettled states, and loudness, the suspected mask, and settledness all vary together.

## 3. The results, graded

"Solid" means the committed artifacts support the claim and I reproduced the numbers today. "Fragile" means supported but carrying a known load-bearing caveat. "Dead" means the artifacts or later work undercut it, whatever its formal status.

### Solid

1. **The 300-window map itself.** All 300 cells measured, 7,435 of 7,500 runs settled, arm classes 47 whole-word, 162 mixed, 91 punctuation funnels, 21 whole-word prompt-dependent cells. I reran the analysis script over the committed shards and reproduced every one of these counts.
2. **The collapse is a one-cell, two-condition phenomenon.** "D" in exactly 1 of 300 windows; abolished by natural loudness (0 of 25 settled, "D" absent, verified in the committed run file) and by moving injection from layer 0 to layer 1. Consequence, stated the way the review stated it: the founding claim "Medium collapses to one empty token", in its unconditional form, is contradicted. The correct claim is conditional: Medium collapses under this specific convention.
3. **The islands result.** The word-forming cells at inject-8 and inject-10 (readout 21) are separated by a non-word cell at inject-9; pre-registered, and the refutation of contiguity survived a disjoint 25-prompt set. The alternation word, not-word, word across injection layers 8, 9, 10 reproduced on prompts the original result had never seen.
4. **The A4 relatedness pass, twice.** The three terminal words of the inject-10 read-21 window ("until", "forever", "since") are more geometrically related in the model's word-vector space than 10,000 random word triples matched on superficial features (leading space, length, frequency band): 4.69 standard deviations above the matched-chance average, with about 9 in 10,000 random triples scoring as high. An earlier, silently deleted run of the same control (restored to the record this week, see section 4) found the same thing independently at 4.59 standard deviations. Both files verified today. Equally solid and equally important: the other word window, O8, shows nothing (it sits at its chance average, 0.08 standard deviations below it), so word relatedness is a property of one cell, not of the word zone.
5. **The medium model has no coherent lens band, as scoped.** Zero of 23 layers pass the pre-registered rule; no lens-side trace of the islands. The scope limit is real, though: the census scored only the prediction position (the readout regime the instrument's own validation gate found weakest), and a content-position variant was never run and is cheap.
6. **Window effects are generic.** The Pythia placebo grid shows strong window-position effects on a model with no consolidation, so window dependence alone is not evidence of workspace structure. Its depth verdict (looping only the first half of the stack makes Pythia consolidate where the full stack fragments, 25 of 25 versus 13 of 25 settling, verified today) is solid as recorded but carries a loudness confound: the two arms injected at 63 times and 403 times natural strength respectively, so depth and loudness co-vary.
7. **The small model's landscape lives only in the full stack.** The window grid on GPT-2 Small found no sub-window that carries the Stage 1 landscape; every cut destroys the semantic basins. The five wrongly transcribed descriptive numbers in that results file were corrected this week against the committed artifact; I re-checked the corrected values today and they match the artifact exactly.

### Fragile

1. **The hidden Small-likeness under the mask (score 0.200, p = 0.0009).** This is the observation the mask hypothesis leans on, and it is real in the committed file, but it rests on one prompt subset, a reference partition in which 7 of 25 prompts never settled, and a cluster structure in which 18 of the 25 prompts sit in one cluster. Its decisive follow-up (the natural-loudness comparison, issue #59) has not run.
2. **The word windows as findings about the model.** They survive seed variation, a disjoint prompt subset, a change of the technical attachment point used to splice the signal in, and natural loudness, which is genuinely strong. But they rest on one readout family; the second instrument sees nothing at those layers; and the record's own proposed stopping-rule stability check at those two exact cells was never run. The reason that check matters is now on the record: at the slow-drift cell inject-9 read-21, merely changing when the loop is allowed to stop changed the terminal word on 5 of 5 tested prompts (verified today in the settle-time file). Until the same check runs at the flagship cells, their specific terminal words are stopping-rule-conditional in principle.
3. **The band-window refutation (the register's H11).** The result that the escaped window does not recreate the small model's geometry is numerically verified and robust across thresholds, but its specification, its analysis code, and its results entered the record in a single commit, so its pre-registration rests on testimony rather than on commit order. The mitigating fact is that it refutes the programme's own hoped-for outcome.
4. **The political-vocabulary work on the unmerged pull request #53.** A held-out test (EXP_014) found that feeding the small model's settled "prolet" state back as input pulls held-out socialist vocabulary from median rank 15,998 of 50,257 up to rank 70, and a companion analysis claims the medium model has a political attractor of its own (a "Republican" terminal at one window, 25 of 25 under both readouts on its branch). I spot-checked both numbers on the branch today. All of it is single-readout, partly unreviewed, and none of it is on the main branch; the review's disposition (split, rename, and gate it) stands and is on your decision list.

### Dead

1. **The naive band mapping that chose the original window grid** (layers 10 to 21 as the presumed workspace). No instrument found anything resembling it.
2. **The expectation that the two instruments would localise the same band.** Recorded on 2026-07-23 as the falsifiable prediction; it is falsified. The looping procedure and the lens disagree about whether anything at all distinguishes layers 8 and 10 from layer 9.
3. **The hypothesis that the escaped window recreates the small model's geometry.** Refuted at every clustering threshold: agreement scores of 0.01 to 0.07 on the 0-to-1 scale, with at least 1 in 6 random regroupings scoring as well at every threshold (verified today).
4. **The coarse window-dependence hypothesis as workspace evidence.** Its letter survived (windows differ, trivially confirmed), but the placebo grid showed window dependence is generic, so it carries no workspace meaning.
5. **The unconditional founding claim.** As above: "Medium collapses to one empty token" is true only of one cut under one loudness convention. The project's most-quoted sentence needs the conditional attached every time it is said aloud.
6. **The "readout depth 21 is the only reliable depth" prior.** Superseded by the full census (6.6 of 25 average agreement at depth 21 across eleven cells, versus 18.9 of 25 at depth 22).
7. **The plan's kill criteria as written.** The registered rule for abandoning the workspace framing begins with a condition ("the window experiment shows no qualitative window-dependence") that is already false, so the rule can never fire, in either direction. The framing currently cannot be killed or saved by its own registered test, which is a register defect, not a scientific result. Re-registration is on your decision list.

## 4. The register cleanup

### What went wrong

The project runs on pre-registration: the prediction and the scoring rule are committed to the repository before the experiment runs, so results cannot be quietly reinterpreted afterwards. That discipline requires that hypothesis numbers and experiment names mean one thing each. Two incidents broke that.

First, the collision that became issue #52. Two automated sessions, working concurrently on branches invisible to each other, each pre-registered the same in-fill experiment on 2026-07-24, under different hypothesis numbers with differently worded criteria. The runs themselves were fine (the protocol is deterministic, and the two sides' outputs are bit-for-bit identical, which is determinism, not replication). But three hypothesis numbers ended up meaning two different things each, and under two of them the two meanings carried opposite verdict words, so the record could assert "supported" and "refuted" under one name. The dispute over which registration governs sat open while other work piled on top of it.

Second, an incident of the same class found by this review, worse in one respect: the anisotropy-corrected permutation control (the matched-chance word-relatedness test in section 3) was also registered twice and run twice, and at a merge on 2026-07-25 the first run's entire results section was silently deleted from the record, with no supersession marker anywhere. What was deleted was concordant with the surviving run (the same 4.6-standard-deviation result on the same window), so what was hidden was favourable evidence, but the house convention that superseded material stays visible was violated either way.

The root causes, per the review: concurrent sessions with no shared ledger of claimed identifiers, no mandatory claim step before starting work, and merges that moved faster than reading.

### What was done

All of the following landed on the main branch on 2026-07-31 via pull request #57, after the operator merged the census pull request #10 earlier the same day, and issue #52 is now closed.

1. `_STAGE2_JSPACE/REGISTER.md` now exists and is the authoritative in-repo register of every hypothesis number and experiment name, with the canonical binding, the retired binding, the verdict, and the git hash for each; retired bindings stay visible and are never reused; the next free hypothesis number is declared (H15).
2. The adjudication follows the 2026-07-26 registry ruling: the main branch's numbering is canonical, the census hypothesis was renumbered to avoid the clash, and the parallel registration's full write-up survives in the results record marked superseded, not deleted.
3. The deleted permutation run was restored: its results section is back in the record verbatim as a marked archival restoration, and its data file is back in the tree under a dated name. I verified today that the restored file's numbers match the restored prose and are concordant with the run of record.
4. The registry ruling's one factual error (it declared a sub-hypothesis vacated that in fact lives on the main branch with a recorded verdict) is corrected in the register's errata, with a correcting comment posted to the registry thread.
5. The five wrong descriptive numbers in the small-model grid results were corrected against their artifact, and the peer-board skill's founding-incident description (which over-counted the collision) was fixed.

### What now prevents recurrence, and what does not yet

The register file carries the working rule: no new hypothesis or experiment identifier may be used in any spec or results file without a row added to the register in the same commit, and the board thread is demoted to a mirror of the file. That rule, plus the visible-supersession convention, is what currently stands between the project and a third incident. What does not yet exist is machine enforcement: there is no continuous-integration check that fails a pull request using an unregistered identifier, no standing rules file for agents (the reviewed rulebook R1 to R8 with your two obligations T1 and T2 remains a recommendation on an unmerged branch), no mandatory claim step, and no branch hygiene (20 stale working branches still exist on the remote as of today). Prevention is presently by convention, and conventions are what failed twice. Making it mechanical is on your decision list.
